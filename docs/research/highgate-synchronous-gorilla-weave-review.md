# Thirteen club synchronous gorilla weave: source review

## Source confirmation

The pattern is described on 2017 printed p. 330 (PDF p. 331) and 2014 printed/PDF p. 238. The 2014 edition is `passing.zone/wp-content/uploads/highgate.pdf`; the expanded 2017 edition is `jugglingedge.com/pdf/highgate.pdf`. Both pages contain the same descriptive text and a causal-diagram graphic. The inventory previously marked the 2014 entry absent; it now records the matching body page.

The source text establishes these facts:

- Ben starts with four clubs, half a beat before the other jugglers.
- Ben feeds on synchronous one-count, throwing singles to the weavers' left hands.
- He passes four times to each weaver across the forward, front, and backward portions of their walk.
- Anne, Clare, and Daniel pass three times to Ben on two-count, then pass on six-count while walking at the back.

These are phases of one walking pattern. The page's causal graphic contains two distinct stacked charts, so they must not be collapsed into one repeating graph without determining the phase boundary and transition.

## Diagram inspection and unresolved evidence

The 2017 source embeds the chart as a 463 x 238 raster image (PDF image object 2339); the 2014 source also embeds it as a 463 x 238 raster image (PDF image object 1538). Extracted images are pixel-identical. Rendering either PDF at higher DPI only interpolates those pixels. There are no source vector paths to recover. Enlarging the 2014 render makes the rows and many lines visible but does not make the ambiguous crossings around Ben's simultaneous right/left tracks and the Anne/Clare/Daniel lanes unambiguous.

The visible layout has four actors, alternating-hand tracks for Anne, Clare, and Daniel, and simultaneous R/L event pairs for Ben, with a half-beat phase lead. Each chart displays 13 nodes on each of Anne, Clare, and Daniel's tracks and seven paired R/L positions for Ben, including what appear to be boundary repeats. The two charts differ in their edge routing.

The accompanying text establishes two changing rhythms for the three weavers: two-count passes to Ben, followed by six-count passes at the back. It is plausible that the charts are the two periodic states for those phases, but the charts are unlabeled and do not mark the transition. A visual hypothesis is that the lower chart is the denser two-count state and the upper chart the later six-count state; this has not been independently established and must not be used as transcription evidence.

The unresolved endpoints cluster around Ben's simultaneous R/L pairs from the third displayed pair onward, where diagonals between Ben and the Anne/Clare/Daniel tracks cross or merge at this resolution. In the upper chart, this includes the transitions around Clare's middle events and Daniel's first pass events; in the lower chart it includes Ben's R/L arrivals from Clare and Daniel and their crossings with Anne's pass lines. The ambiguous labels are therefore the exact receiver (juggler, hand-event ordinal) of those crossed paths, and whether the path reaches the current or next displayed cycle. No complete throw-to-catch endpoint and cycle-offset list can be certified from the source image. The row count, source text, and pixel comparison alone do not establish those details. No graph record has been written from inferred endpoints.

The *Passing Patterns Compendium* has a related split one-count speed-weave description (feedees change from quicker passing rhythms to six-count while moving behind the pattern). This supports the interpretation that the Highgate entry has distinct cadence phases, but it is not the same named pattern and does not provide its edge list.

## Review result

The source and phase mechanics are confirmed, but the diagrams are not yet transcribed. Keep `highgate-thirteenclubsynchronousgorillaweave` graph-transcription-pending. The most direct evidence needed is an original higher-resolution chart, an editable/vector source, or an author-verified edge list that labels the two phase diagrams and their cycle boundary. A video could clarify the cadence and phase order, but not by itself prove every causal arrow endpoint. Until the diagram endpoints are independently legible, do not import a guessed graph into the editor catalog.
